Time

One may get ahead in life.

How much time has passed is just an estimate made by your brain, often convoluted by distractions and other “worldly” activities.

We humans are so obsessed with the future that we can’t escape death, and time is born in the quest to defy that truth. Time is an emergent property in our abstraction of the universe (the platform we are instantiated in) because of our realization of our own mortality (game over). We have found a way to quantify this for our day-to-day use, and that is what everyone thinks time is. Now we look at the universe with this fundamental idea being a core one—a restriction, rather. Our collective ideas evolve; we are constantly learning and reinforcing, as cells, as individuals, as a society, as a species. We can do whatever needs to be done.

Time is a manifestation of our imagination. Our large societies would collapse without time as a concept. The pursuit to quantify time has been ongoing forever; even now we want to measure it with more precision. More precision allows for more data points in the simulation domain. But that is merely for engineering purposes. One could technically quantify love based on neuron firing and heart rhythm and beats, and by training that data on neural networks. One could possibly get this done, but is that a true depiction of love? At my work we sell Digital Experiences (the good and expensive kind), which we quantify as collections of processes.

The past is inaccessible directly and the future is uncertain, so all we have is the current time. But we cannot explicitly pinpoint a particle at a precise time (uncertainty principle). This is almost like a paradox in our interpretation of time and hence “the universe.” The universe has been in motion (since we have realized it). It has to regenerate itself every Planck time (that’s the smallest time we can measure and hence also the smallest distance); so do we, and so does all other fundamental stuff (we are still unsure what that is—what is that thing that was there 5.39×10-44 of a second after the big bang, the “bits” of the universe?). If we are a biological computer, which we are, time is the duration of the usage of the computer, and this computer became sentient—which we will define some other day.
